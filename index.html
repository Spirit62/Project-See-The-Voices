who we are

We got tired of walking past people who couldn't be heard.

Started small. A few students, one school, one session. We noticed the deaf and hard of hearing community around us was invisible — not because they weren't there, but because nobody had learned to look.

So we started learning sign language. Then teaching it. One workshop at a time.

We're not an NGO. Just students who thought this mattered.

The Board

Real words. Real students. Real feelings.

I didn't know silence could feel this loud.

Went home and taught my mum three signs.

I kept messing up the signs and nobody cared. That was the point.

Never thought about what it costs to not be understood.

The room felt different after. Quieter but not empty.

One hour and something just — shifted.

I keep catching myself looking at people's hands now.

We were all bad at it together. It was kind of nice.

Didn't expect to feel anything. Then I did.

My cousin is deaf. I've never been able to talk to her properly. Working on it.

Stayed after to practice. Missed my bus. Worth it.

Just made me pay attention. That's enough.
